Hitachi Semiconductor Reverse Engineering

Legacy MCU and ASIC reverse engineering – H8, SH, and more

Brand Overview

Hitachi was a major Japanese semiconductor manufacturer, now merged into Renesas Electronics, producing a wide range of microcontrollers including the famous H8, SH, and HD series. The H8 family was widely used in automotive, consumer, and industrial applications, while the SuperH (SH) architecture was used in many embedded systems and even Sega consoles. Hitachi also made custom ASICs for numerous Japanese manufacturers, and many legacy systems still rely on Hitachi chips, especially in automotive ECUs, where replacement parts are often unavailable, making reverse engineering essential.

We have extensive experience with Hitachi’s microcontrollers and ASICs, extracting firmware from masked ROM, flash, and OTP devices, as well as recovering data from locked or password‑protected devices. Our expertise covers the H8/300 and H8/500 series, SH‑2, SH‑3, SH‑4, and custom Hitachi DSP chips used in audio and communications. We provide cloning services to replicate these chips exactly, allowing clients to continue production without redesigning their systems, and we also offer firmware analysis to understand the underlying algorithms.

Our services are confidential with strict NDAs, and we have successfully reverse‑engineered Hitachi chips for automotive and medical clients using both non‑invasive and invasive methods. We have a high success rate even on very old devices, and we can assist in migrating Hitachi code to modern platforms. Our team includes experts who have worked with Hitachi since the 1990s, understanding the intricacies of their instruction sets and peripheral design.

Security Features & Reverse Engineering Insights

Hitachi microcontrollers often implement security via ID codes and memory lock bits. To access the device, a correct ID must be presented during the programming mode; we have discovered that these IDs can often be recovered by brute‑forcing or logic analysis. The memory lock bits prevent readback of the internal flash or ROM, but we can bypass these locks using glitch attacks on the reset pin, causing the chip to enter a test mode that ignores security, after which we can dump the memory using a custom JTAG or serial interface.

For masked ROM devices, there is no way to read the code through standard interfaces; we use decapsulation and micro‑probing to read the ROM content directly, removing the packaging and using a microscope to see the bits. We have successfully extracted code from H8 and SH masked ROMs. Some Hitachi chips have hardware encryption for critical data; we can attack the encryption using side‑channel analysis, measuring power consumption during encryption to recover the key, allowing us to decrypt protected firmware and data. We have also developed a technique to clone Hitachi chips with unique IDs, reading the ID and programming it into a new device to ensure authentication.

Our experience includes working with Hitachi's proprietary debug interfaces, which we have reverse‑engineered to gain access. We can also extract calibration data and trim values from OTP, which are essential for accurate replacement. We provide a complete analysis of the extracted firmware, including identifying functions, variables, and algorithms, and we can reconstruct the original source code in C. Our work is crucial for maintaining legacy medical equipment and automotive ECUs, and we have a proven track record of success with Hitachi parts. All projects are handled with utmost confidentiality, and we can provide references upon request.
