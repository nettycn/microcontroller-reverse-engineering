About SyncMOS

SyncMOS is a Taiwanese semiconductor company known for its 8051‑compatible microcontrollers and low‑power SoCs. Their products are used in consumer electronics, industrial automation, and automotive applications. SyncMOS offers a wide range of MCUs with integrated flash memory, EEPROM, and SRAM. They are recognized for their cost‑effective solutions and reliable performance. SyncMOS provides both general‑purpose and application‑specific devices, including motor control and battery management chips. Their MCUs support multiple communication interfaces like UART, SPI, I2C, and CAN. SyncMOS also offers development tools and reference designs. They have a strong presence in the Asian market, particularly in China and Taiwan. SyncMOS's products are designed for low power consumption, making them ideal for portable devices. They offer a variety of package options, from DIP to QFN. SyncMOS also produces secure microcontrollers with hardware encryption. Their devices are used in smart meters, home appliances, and medical equipment. SyncMOS has a reputation for high quality and long product lifecycles. They provide excellent technical support and documentation. SyncMOS is also a supplier of touch‑key controllers and LED drivers. They are continuously expanding their product portfolio with new ARM‑based offerings. SyncMOS's microcontrollers are known for their robustness in industrial environments. They offer both commercial and industrial temperature ranges. SyncMOS has a dedicated R&D team focused on low‑power and high‑performance designs. They are an ISO 9001 certified company. SyncMOS also provides custom silicon services for high‑volume customers. Their competitive pricing makes them a popular choice for cost‑sensitive projects. SyncMOS's legacy 8051 parts are still widely used in many legacy systems. The company remains committed to supporting older products with long‑term availability.

SyncMOS Security Features

SyncMOS implements basic security features to protect firmware from unauthorized access. Their 8051‑based devices include lock bits that prevent readback of the program memory. These bits are programmed via a dedicated command sequence. Once set, they block any read attempts from the debug interface. However, these locks can be bypassed using glitch attacks on the power supply or clock line. Some newer SyncMOS MCUs offer a hardware encryption engine to decrypt the firmware during operation. The encryption key is stored in a secure register that cannot be read out. This makes it difficult to extract the plaintext code without the key. SyncMOS also provides a unique chip ID to prevent cloning and duplication. Their secure boot feature verifies the firmware integrity using a CRC or signature. This ensures that only authenticated code is executed, making it harder to hack the device. Fault injection techniques have been used to bypass these checks in older models. Side‑channel power analysis can reveal the encryption key during cryptographic operations. Our lab has successfully recovered keys from SM32F series using differential electromagnetic analysis. We have also used laser fault injection to unlock protected areas and dump the flash. For OTP variants, we can read the contents by decapsulating the chip and using a scanning electron microscope. SyncMOS's security is generally adequate for consumer applications but may not withstand determined attacks. They have introduced tamper detection and active shielding in some new products. These features make invasive extraction more difficult. Nevertheless, our team has developed advanced methods to attack even the latest SyncMOS devices. We offer both non‑invasive (power analysis, glitching) and invasive (FIB, probing) extraction services. We can duplicate the firmware onto a new chip or recover it for analysis. Our success rate is high, and we maintain strict confidentiality. Contact us for a thorough evaluation of your SyncMOS MCU.
