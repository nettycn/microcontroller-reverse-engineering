GigaDevice Semiconductor Reverse Engineering

Expert services for GigaDevice GD32 ARM Cortex-M MCUs and SPI NOR Flash

Brand Overview

GigaDevice is a Chinese fabless semiconductor company founded in 2005, known for its GD32 series of 32‑bit ARM Cortex‑M microcontrollers and a wide range of SPI NOR flash memory. The GD32 MCUs are pin‑compatible with STM32, offering a cost‑effective alternative, and have gained popularity in consumer electronics, IoT, industrial controls, motor control, human‑machine interfaces, and audio. They also offer RISC‑V based MCUs under the GD32V series, among the first mass‑produced RISC‑V devices.

Their flash memory includes security features like write protection and unique ID, but like many Chinese vendors, security implementation can be variable. We have reverse‑engineered many GD32 chips for clients needing legacy support, extracting firmware from locked GD32 MCUs using advanced techniques. We also recover data from GigaDevice SPI flash chips, and our services include full firmware extraction, analysis, cloning, and unique ID spoofing, which is useful for reproducing systems that rely on authentication.

We have helped customers migrate from STM32 to GD32 by reverse engineering the firmware, and our expertise covers all GD32 families, from F1 to F4 and later. We also provide training on GD32 security assessment, and with MikaTech, you can overcome supply chain issues using cloned GD32 chips, ensuring high quality and fast turnaround.

Security Features & Reverse Engineering Insights

GigaDevice MCUs implement security features similar to STM32, including read‑out protection (RDP). RDP level 1 prevents reading flash via debug interface, while level 2 disables debug permanently. We have developed methods to bypass RDP on GD32 chips using glitch attacks – by manipulating the supply voltage during reset, we can force the chip to skip the protection check, allowing us to read back the entire flash content. We can also use option byte manipulation to unlock the device, and we have successfully extracted firmware from GD32F1, F2, F3, and even V series.

The flash memory also has write protection to prevent unauthorized modifications; we can bypass this by temporarily disabling it using a similar glitch. For GD32V RISC‑V chips, the security model is similar but with a different boot sequence; we have reverse‑engineered the boot ROM to understand its security decisions, which has allowed us to create a generic unlock tool for GD32 devices. Our side‑channel analysis can recover encryption keys used in secure boot, and we can also clone the unique device ID to replicate authenticated devices – crucial for systems that bind firmware to a specific chip.

For GigaDevice SPI flash, we can read the contents even if the protection bits are set, using both software and hardware methods. We can also recover data from flash chips that are partially damaged. Our experience spans multiple generations and package types, and we have developed a library of known vulnerabilities for quick access, reducing project time. We provide detailed documentation of the extraction process, and we can re‑engineer the firmware to port it to other platforms. Our team is proficient in ARM and RISC‑V assembly, and we can identify and extract algorithms, constants, and configuration data. With a high success rate and cost‑effective services, MikaTech is your trusted partner for GD32 reverse engineering.
