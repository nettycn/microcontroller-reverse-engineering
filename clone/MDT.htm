About MDT

MDT (Microchip Design Technology) is a Taiwanese manufacturer of low‑cost microcontrollers, primarily 8051‑compatible cores. Their devices are widely used in consumer electronics, toys, and home appliances. MDT offers both OTP (One‑Time Programmable) and flash‑based variants. They are known for high volume production and competitive pricing. MDT MCUs often include built‑in RC oscillators and low‑voltage detectors. Their product line includes general‑purpose and application‑specific chips. MDT provides development tools and programmers for their MCUs. They are a popular choice for budget‑sensitive projects in Asia. MDT’s microcontrollers are pin‑compatible with many industry‑standard 8051 devices. They offer a range of packages from SOP to QFN. MDT’s OTP parts are ideal for fixed‑function firmware with no need for updates. Their flash variants allow in‑system programming for flexibility. MDT also produces EEPROM‑based MCUs for parameter storage. Their chips are used in remote controls, battery chargers, and small motors. MDT provides application notes and reference designs. They focus on simplicity and ease of integration. MDT’s devices operate over a wide temperature range. They have a reputation for reliable performance in noisy environments. MDT also offers custom firmware development services. Their microcontrollers are supported by third‑party assemblers and C compilers. MDT continues to innovate with new low‑power sleep modes. They add features like watchdog timers and comparators. MDT’s commitment to quality ensures long product lifecycles. Many legacy products rely on MDT chips, creating demand for reverse engineering. Their devices are often used in white goods and smart home devices. MDT is a key player in the Asian semiconductor market. Their products are cost‑effective alternatives to larger brands.

MDT Security Features

MDT implements basic security features typical of low‑cost 8051 designs. The primary protection is a security fuse that, when programmed, prevents readback of the code memory. This fuse is a single bit in a configuration register. Once set, the device cannot be read via the parallel programming interface. However, this lock can be bypassed using a glitch on the VPP pin. Many MDT OTP parts have no additional security, making them relatively easy to hack. To extract firmware, we often use a combination of voltage manipulation and clock glitching. Our lab has successfully dumped thousands of MDT devices. The process involves inserting a specific timing violation to unlock the protection. Once unlocked, we can read out the entire memory and save it as a binary file. For OTP, the data is permanent, so we must duplicate it onto a flash‑based MDT or another compatible chip. MDT also offers a password protection scheme on some flash devices. The password is stored in a special sector and must be verified for programming access. Cracking this password is feasible through brute‑force or side‑channel analysis. We have developed tools to attack the password mechanism. MDT’s security is generally weak compared to modern MCUs. However, many legacy systems still depend on these chips, and their firmware is often lost. We offer recovery and cloning services to keep these systems running. Our non‑invasive methods minimize damage and preserve the chip. We can also recover data from damaged or partially erased devices. MDT’s simplicity works in our favor, making extraction straightforward. For clients, we provide the extracted code in HEX and ASM formats. We can also analyze the code to understand its functionality. Trust MikaTech for all your MDT reverse engineering needs.
