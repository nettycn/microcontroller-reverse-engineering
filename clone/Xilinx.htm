About Xilinx

Xilinx is the world leader in FPGA and adaptive SoC technology, acquired by AMD in 2022. Their Zynq series integrates ARM Cortex‑A cores with programmable logic. These devices are not traditional microcontrollers but are highly capable embedded systems. Xilinx also offers the MicroBlaze soft‑core processor, which can be implemented inside the FPGA fabric. They are widely used in aerospace, defense, automotive, and high‑performance computing. Zynq‑7000 and Zynq UltraScale+ are flagship families. Xilinx provides a complete software stack including PetaLinux and Vitis tools. Their devices support multiple operating systems like Linux, FreeRTOS, and bare‑metal. Xilinx’s programmable logic allows custom hardware accelerators. They are popular in vision processing, 5G infrastructure, and motor control. Xilinx also provides secure boot with RSA authentication. They have integrated cryptographic engines for encryption and decryption. Xilinx chips include battery‑backed RAM for key storage. Their devices have a unique ID for device authentication. Xilinx offers bitstream encryption to protect IP. The company has a strong ecosystem of third‑party IP cores. Xilinx FPGAs are used in medical imaging and radar systems. They also support high‑speed interfaces like PCIe, Ethernet, and USB. Xilinx’s development tools include simulation and debug capabilities. They provide extensive documentation and training resources. Xilinx’s acquisition by AMD broadened their compute portfolio. Their processors are known for high performance and low latency. Xilinx also offers radiation‑tolerant versions for space. The company has a global support network. Xilinx devices are often used in prototyping and production. Their flexibility allows rapid iteration of design. Xilinx’s commitment to security is evident in their secure boot and key management. They are a key player in the embedded and edge AI market. Xilinx continues to innovate with Versal adaptive compute acceleration platforms.

Xilinx Security Features

Xilinx implements robust security mechanisms to protect both the FPGA bitstream and the ARM firmware. The Zynq series includes a secure boot process that uses RSA signatures to authenticate the first‑stage bootloader. If the signature is invalid, the device refuses to boot, preventing unauthorized code execution. The bitstream can be encrypted with AES‑256, making it impossible to readback without the key. The decryption key is stored in a battery‑backed RAM or eFuse, which is physically secure. Xilinx also provides a TrustZone environment for ARM cores, isolating secure and non‑secure software. The programmable logic can be configured to lock out JTAG access after programming. However, advanced attackers may attempt glitch attacks to bypass these protections. Fault injection can sometimes disable the secure boot check, allowing an attacker to hack into the system. Side‑channel power analysis can be used to recover the encryption key during boot. We have successfully extracted keys from Zynq devices using differential power analysis. Another method involves probing the configuration interface during power‑up. Xilinx devices have a unique DNA that can be used for device authentication, making it harder to clone. Despite these measures, we have developed techniques to dump the bitstream from the external configuration flash. Once the bitstream is recovered, we can duplicate the design onto another FPGA. For the ARM firmware, we can read the QSPI flash and extract the software. If the security fuses are blown, invasive techniques like FIB can be used to re‑enable debug. We have also used electromagnetic analysis to infer internal states during crypto operations. Xilinx’s security is strong, but not infallible. We have recovered firmware from locked Zynq devices for clients. Our team can also unlock the JTAG interface through voltage glitching. Once unlocked, we can read the entire OCM and DDR contents.
