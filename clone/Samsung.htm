About Samsung

Samsung Electronics is a global technology giant, known for its consumer electronics, memory, and semiconductor solutions. Their microcontroller portfolio includes ARM Cortex‑M based chips, legacy 8051 cores, and custom SoCs for specific applications. Samsung’s MCUs are used in their own products like smartphones, TVs, and home appliances, as well as sold to external customers. They are known for high integration, combining processing, memory, and connectivity on a single chip. Samsung also produces secure microcontrollers for smart cards and payment applications. Their Exynos processors are powerful application processors, but they also offer simpler MCUs for IoT. Samsung’s manufacturing capabilities allow them to produce advanced geometry MCUs with low power. Their products are often used in consumer electronics, automotive, and industrial controls. Samsung provides development tools and support for their MCUs. They have a strong R&D focus on AI and machine learning at the edge. Samsung’s security solutions include secure boot, encryption, and secure key storage. They also offer unique device IDs for authentication. Their microcontrollers often feature advanced analog and digital peripherals. Samsung is a major player in the global semiconductor market. Their MCUs are used in many third‑party products. They have a strong presence in Korea and global markets. Samsung’s product lifecycles are typically long, supporting legacy systems. They continue to innovate with new materials and packaging technologies. Samsung is also a leader in DRAM and NAND flash, which often integrate with their MCUs. They provide comprehensive documentation and reference designs. Samsung’s commitment to quality is reflected in their rigorous testing. They are a trusted supplier for many industries.

Samsung Security Features

Samsung incorporates strong security measures in their MCUs, especially for payment and identity applications. They feature a dedicated cryptographic processor for AES, RSA, and ECC. This allows encrypted firmware to be decrypted during boot, preventing unauthorized readback. The keys are stored in a tamper‑sensitive memory that self‑destructs on intrusion. Samsung provides secure boot with signature verification. Debug ports are locked with a password and can be permanently disabled. They also include active shielding to prevent micro‑probing. However, attackers may attempt to hack the device using fault injection. Side‑channel power analysis can reveal keys during cryptographic operations. Our team has successfully extracted firmware from Samsung smart card MCUs using electromagnetic analysis. We have also used glitch attacks to bypass security on older S3C8 devices. Invasive decapsulation allows direct reading of the flash. Samsung’s security is robust, but no system is perfect. We have developed methods to recover data from locked chips. We can clone the device by reading the die. Samsung’s ongoing security improvements mean we must stay current. MikaTech provides comprehensive reverse engineering services for Samsung MCUs.
