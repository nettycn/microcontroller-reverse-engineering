About STC

STC (Shenzhen STC Microelectronics) is a well‑known Chinese manufacturer of 8051‑compatible microcontrollers. Their products are widely used in consumer electronics, industrial control, and smart home devices. STC MCUs are famous for their high speed, low power, and large on‑chip memory. They offer a wide range of devices from 8‑bit to 32‑bit ARM‑based cores. STC's 8051 designs are fully compatible with the standard Intel instruction set. They integrate flash memory, EEPROM, and SRAM on a single chip. STC also provides advanced peripherals like CAN, LIN, and USB. Their microcontrollers are known for their excellent performance in motor control and power management. STC has a strong market presence in China and is expanding globally. They offer comprehensive development tools, including C compilers and debuggers. STC's products are used in LED lighting, security systems, and medical devices. They provide both industrial and automotive‑grade temperature ranges. STC continuously innovates with new low‑power and high‑speed designs. They also produce secure microcontrollers with encryption and authentication. STC has a dedicated engineering team for customer support. Their devices are available in many package types, from SOP to QFN. STC's competitive pricing makes them popular for high‑volume applications. They also offer OTP (One‑Time Programmable) options for cost‑sensitive designs. STC's MCUs are often used in white goods and power tools. They have a large library of application notes and reference designs. STC is an ISO 9001 and IATF 16949 certified company. They have partnerships with major foundries for manufacturing. STC's 32‑bit ARM‑based series offer high performance for complex applications. Their products are widely adopted in the automotive aftermarket. STC's focus on quality and reliability has earned them a loyal customer base. They continue to invest in R&D for future technologies.

STC Security Features

STC incorporates security features to protect its microcontrollers from unauthorised access. Their 8051‑based devices include lock bits that, when set, prevent readback of the flash memory. These bits are programmed via a special command sequence. Once locked, the debug interface is disabled, and the code cannot be read out. However, voltage glitching can sometimes temporarily unlock these bits, allowing extraction. Some STC MCUs offer a hardware encryption module that can decrypt the firmware during execution. The decryption key is stored in a secure OTP area that cannot be read. This makes it extremely difficult to extract the plaintext firmware without the key. STC also provides a unique device ID for anti‑cloning purposes. Their secure boot feature verifies the firmware signature, ensuring only authenticated code runs. This prevents attackers from injecting malicious code. Fault injection attacks, such as clock or power glitching, have been used to bypass these checks in some older models. Side‑channel analysis via power consumption can reveal encryption keys. Our team has successfully recovered keys from STC32F series using differential power analysis. We have also used laser fault injection to alter the program counter and dump the flash. For legacy 89C51 parts, we have developed methods to unlock the lock bits by applying a specific voltage sequence. This allows us to recover the full firmware. STC's newer devices include tamper detection and active shielding to resist invasive probing. Despite these, we have managed to extract data using focused ion beam (FIB) editing. We can also clone the entire chip by reading out the die. STC's security is adequate for consumer applications but can be broken with sufficient effort. Our lab offers both non‑invasive (side‑channel, glitching) and invasive (decapsulation, probing) extraction services. We can recover the firmware and duplicate it onto a new device. Our success rate is high, and we maintain strict confidentiality. Contact us for a detailed assessment of your STC MCU. We have extensive experience with this brand and can handle even the most protected devices.
