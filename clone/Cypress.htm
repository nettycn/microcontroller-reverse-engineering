About Cypress (Now Infineon)

Cypress Semiconductor, now part of Infineon Technologies, was a leading provider of advanced embedded solutions. They are known for their Programmable System-on-Chip (PSoC) devices that combine a microcontroller with programmable analog and digital blocks. Cypress also developed the FM series of ARM Cortex-M based MCUs for automotive and industrial applications, as well as the Traveo family for body electronics and instrument clusters. Their USB controllers and Bluetooth solutions are also widely used. Cypress devices are found in consumer electronics, automotive, industrial control, and IoT. The PSoC series is particularly popular for its flexibility, allowing designers to create custom peripherals. Cypress incorporated robust security features, including secure boot, flash encryption, and tamper detection. They also offered a unique device ID and cryptographic accelerators. However, like all MCUs, they are vulnerable to reverse engineering attacks. Our team has extensive experience with Cypress devices, including PSoC 1, 3, 4, 5LP, and 6, as well as the FM0, FM3, FM4, and Traveo families. We have developed specialized tools to communicate with their programming interfaces (SWD, JTAG, and proprietary protocols). We have successfully extracted firmware from locked PSoC devices by exploiting the bootloader and using power analysis. For the FM series, we use similar techniques, including voltage glitching and electromagnetic fault injection. We also offer decapsulation services for the most stubborn devices. Our clients have used our services to recover lost source code, clone production devices, and analyze competitor products. We provide complete documentation of the extracted firmware and can assist with further analysis. With our expertise, we can decrypt, readback, hack, extract, dump, recover, read, duplicate, and clone Cypress microcontrollers efficiently.

Security Features and Attack Strategies

Cypress devices incorporate a comprehensive security architecture. The PSoC series provides a flash security bit that, when set, blocks external reads through the debug interface. They also have a unique silicon ID that can be used for software protection. For the FM and Traveo families, Infineon provides similar read protection, with additional features like secure boot and encrypted flash. Some devices support a "secure lock" that permanently disables debugging. Cypress also offers a cryptographic engine for encryption and authentication. Despite these strong measures, we have developed many techniques to overcome them. For PSoC devices, we have discovered that the security bit can be bypassed by exploiting the bootloader vulnerability. We can send special commands to put the device into a test mode, allowing us to read the entire flash. For the FM series, we use power analysis to extract the encryption key used for secure boot. Once we have the key, we can decrypt the firmware. We also use fault injection to skip the security check during boot, giving us full access. For devices with no known exploits, we perform decapsulation and direct flash reading. This involves removing the die and using a microscope to read the charge state of each memory cell. This is a costly but effective method. Our team has successfully extracted firmware from all major Cypress families. We also offer cloning, where we take a locked device and produce a working duplicate. This is particularly useful for legacy automotive parts that are no longer available.
