About STK

STK (Shanghai STK Microelectronics) is a Chinese semiconductor company specialising in 8051‑compatible microcontrollers. They offer a range of low‑cost, high‑performance devices for consumer and industrial applications. STK's MCUs are known for their simplicity and ease of use. They provide both flash and OTP (One‑Time Programmable) options. STK products are used in remote controls, toys, and small appliances. They also offer automotive‑qualified parts for body electronics. STK microcontrollers feature integrated flash memory, RAM, and EEPROM. They support standard interfaces like UART, SPI, and I2C. STK provides development tools and programming adapters. Their devices are popular among hobbyists and small‑scale manufacturers. STK also offers custom OTP programming services. They have a strong distribution network in China. STK's MCUs are often pin‑compatible with other 8051 devices. They offer a variety of package types, including SOP and TSSOP. STK's low‑power models are ideal for battery‑operated devices. They also provide temperature‑compensated oscillators for precision timing. STK has a reputation for reliable quality and long‑term availability. They support both assembly and C programming. STK's products are used in smart home devices and environmental monitors. They also supply MCUs for power management systems. STK's commitment to cost‑effectiveness makes them a preferred choice for high‑volume applications. They continuously improve their products with new features. STK also offers secure key storage in some models. Their engineering team provides responsive technical support. STK's future roadmap includes 32‑bit ARM‑based MCUs. Despite being a smaller player, STK has a loyal customer base in Asia. Their products are a cost‑effective alternative to Western brands. STK's OTP parts are widely used in firmware‑fixed designs.

STK Security Features

STK implements basic security features to protect firmware in their MCUs. Their flash devices include lock bits that, when programmed, prevent readback of the code. These bits are stored in configuration memory. Once set, they disable external reading via the programming interface. However, these locks can be bypassed with glitch attacks or by using a specific timing sequence. Some STK devices also offer a hardware encryption option to scramble the firmware, making it unreadable without the key. The key is stored in a secure OTP area. STK provides a unique device ID to deter cloning. Their secure boot option verifies the integrity of the code using a checksum. This prevents execution of tampered code. Fault injection, such as voltage spikes, can sometimes defeat these checks. Side‑channel power analysis has been used to recover keys from STK devices. Our team has successfully extracted keys from STK89C series using differential power analysis. We have also used laser fault injection to unlock protection and dump the flash. For OTP parts, we can read the contents by decapsulating the chip and using optical techniques. STK's security is relatively weak, making them easier to hack compared to major brands. They have added some tamper resistance in newer models, but it is not foolproof. We can clone STK devices by reading the die with a scanning electron microscope. Our services include both non‑invasive and invasive methods. We can recover the firmware and duplicate it onto a new chip. Our success rate is high, and we can handle even OTP devices. STK's security is sufficient for many consumer products. For more critical applications, we recommend additional external security. Contact us for a comprehensive evaluation of your STK MCU.
