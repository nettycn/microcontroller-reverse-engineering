About ICSI

ICSI (Integrated Circuit Solutions, Inc.) is a niche manufacturer of microcontrollers with a focus on memory integration. They produce MCUs that combine flash, EEPROM, and SRAM in high densities. ICSI devices are commonly used in smart cards, SIM cards, and secure identity tokens. They also offer SoC solutions with built‑in security coprocessors. ICSI’s product line includes both ARM and proprietary architectures. They are known for their low‑power designs suitable for battery‑powered devices. ICSI provides extensive cryptographic libraries for their secure MCUs. Their chips are often found in payment terminals and access control systems. ICSI also produces radiation‑hardened versions for aerospace applications. The company emphasizes on‑chip key storage and true random number generation. They have a strong IP portfolio in memory management and error correction. ICSI’s microcontrollers are designed for high reliability and long data retention. They support multiple communication interfaces like I2C, SPI, and UART. ICSI also offers custom silicon services for specialized requirements. Their development tools include debuggers and trace analyzers. ICSI MCUs are often used in medical implants and wearable health monitors. They incorporate advanced power gating and dynamic voltage scaling. ICSI’s security‑focused chips have received Common Criteria certifications. The company collaborates with major OEMs for secure IoT applications. ICSI’s products are also employed in automotive key fobs and immobilizers. They provide firmware update mechanisms with rollback protection. ICSI’s memory technologies include both NOR and NAND flash. Their MCUs can execute code directly from flash with zero‑wait states. ICSI also offers multi‑core solutions for parallel processing. They are an emerging player in the secure microcontroller market. Their devices are competitive with established brands like NXP and STMicroelectronics. ICSI’s roadmap includes post‑quantum cryptography support. They are actively researching hardware‑based security against side‑channel attacks. ICSI’s commitment to innovation makes them a key partner for security‑critical projects.

ICSI Security Features

ICSI integrates robust security at the silicon level to protect sensitive data. Their secure MCUs feature a dedicated crypto engine that can decrypt code on‑the‑fly. This prevents unauthorized readback of firmware as it remains encrypted in flash. The key material is stored in a tamper‑sensitive memory that zeroes out upon detection of physical attack. ICSI also implements active shielding on the die to frustrate micro‑probing. They include a secure boot process that verifies digital signatures before execution. This ensures that only authenticated code runs, making it difficult to hack the system. ICSI’s devices have multiple security levels configurable via fuses. These fuses can permanently disable debug ports after production. To extract data, one might attempt fault injection to unlock these ports. However, ICSI has countermeasures like voltage and frequency monitors. They also incorporate a watchdog timer that resets the chip if anomalies occur. The company provides a secure update mechanism with encrypted binaries. This prevents attackers from injecting malicious payloads. ICSI’s TRNG (True Random Number Generator) adds entropy for cryptographic operations. They also support memory encryption to protect data at rest. Despite these measures, advanced adversaries can still clone the chip by removing the passivation layer and reading out the ROM. Side‑channel power analysis can reveal keys during encryption. Our team has developed techniques to recover keys from ICSI devices using differential electromagnetic analysis. We have also successfully dumped firmware by exploiting a vulnerability in the bootloader. ICSI’s security is strong, but no system is perfect. We offer professional extraction services for legitimate purposes like legacy recovery. Our methods include invasive probing and focused ion beam editing. We can read the contents of protected flash and EEPROM. For OTP variants, we use UV erasing and reprogramming to duplicate the content. ICSI’s chips are used in high‑security environments, so we exercise extreme caution. Our lab is equipped to handle even the most resistant devices.
