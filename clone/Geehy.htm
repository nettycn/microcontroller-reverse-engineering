About Geehy

Geehy (or Geehy Semiconductor) is a Chinese fabless semiconductor company that specializes in 32-bit microcontrollers based on the ARM Cortex-M architecture. Their APM32 series is pin-to-pin compatible with STMicroelectronics' STM32F series, offering an alternative source for many applications. Geehy's MCUs are used in industrial control, consumer electronics, motor drives, and automotive aftermarket. They offer a wide range of products from Cortex-M0 to M4 cores, with various memory sizes and peripheral sets. Geehy also provides integrated solutions with built-in CAN, USB, and Ethernet interfaces. The company aims to provide high-performance, low-power, and cost-effective alternatives. Their devices are widely adopted in the Chinese market and are increasingly found in global products. Geehy includes security features like flash read protection, write protection, and a unique device ID. Some devices have a tamper-proof memory for storing keys. However, their security implementation is often similar to STM32's, with known vulnerabilities. Our reverse engineering team has extensive experience with Geehy APM32 devices. We have successfully extracted firmware from many locked Geehy MCUs using techniques like debug port unlocking, side-channel analysis, and fault injection. We also provide cloning services to duplicate firmware onto new Geehy chips. Our tools are compatible with the standard SWD/JTAG interface, and we have developed custom scripts to automate the extraction process. For highly secured devices, we resort to decapsulation and direct memory reading. Our clients include companies that need to recover lost source code, verify compliance, or produce backups. We offer fast turnaround and high success rates. With our expertise, we can decrypt, readback, hack, extract, dump, recover, read, duplicate, and clone Geehy microcontrollers with confidence.

Security Features and Reverse Engineering Methodology

Geehy's APM32 series incorporates security features that are similar to STM32's. The main protection is the Read Protection (RDP) levels: RDP0 (no protection), RDP1 (read protection enabled, debug access blocked), and RDP2 (full protection, no access). Additionally, they provide write protection for specific flash sectors to prevent accidental overwrites. Some devices have a backup domain with security registers that can lock certain functions. Geehy also includes a unique device ID for anti-cloning. However, these protections have known weaknesses. For RDP1, we can use the debug interface with a special sequence to lift the protection without erasing the flash, a technique we have perfected. For RDP2, which is more secure, we apply voltage fault injection to bypass the protection logic. We have successfully unlocked RDP2 devices by glitching the power supply during the boot sequence. Once unlocked, we can fully access the flash and read the firmware. We also use side-channel power analysis to extract encryption keys stored in the device. For devices with a bootloader that supports firmware upgrades, we can sometimes exploit the bootloader's protocol to read the main flash. We have also developed custom firmware that can be injected into the RAM to read the flash via a backdoor. When non-invasive methods fail, we resort to decapsulation and microprobing to read the flash memory directly. This is done by removing the packaging and using a scanning electron microscope to image the floating gates. We have a high success rate with all these approaches. Our lab is equipped with advanced equipment for both non-invasive and invasive attacks. We also provide cloning services, where we take a locked device, extract the firmware, and program a blank Geehy chip. This is especially useful when the original device is obsolete or no longer available. Our team stays updated with the latest security updates from Geehy, and we continuously refine our tools. We have successfully attacked many APM32F and APM32L devices. Our services are performed with strict confidentiality and under NDA.
