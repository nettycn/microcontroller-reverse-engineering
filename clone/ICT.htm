About ICT

ICT (Integrated Circuit Technology) is a lesser‑known but significant manufacturer of microcontrollers, primarily focused on 8051‑compatible cores. They supply cost‑effective OTP (One‑Time Programmable) MCUs for consumer electronics and appliances. ICT devices are often found in remote controls, toys, and small household gadgets. Their product line includes both mask ROM and flash‑based variants. ICT MCUs are known for their low power consumption and small footprint. They offer a range of packages from DIP to QFN. ICT also provides development tools and programmers for their chips. The company targets high‑volume, low‑cost applications. Their 8051 cores are fully compatible with standard Intel instruction sets. ICT enhances their designs with additional peripherals like PWM and comparators. They have a reputation for reliable operation in noisy environments. ICT’s OTP devices are popular for fixed‑function firmware where updates are not needed. They also produce EEPROM‑based microcontrollers for parameter storage. ICT’s product portfolio includes both general‑purpose and application‑specific chips. Their MCUs are often used in power supplies, battery chargers, and motor drives. ICT provides application notes and sample code for common tasks. The company’s focus on simplicity makes their devices easy to integrate. ICT also offers custom firmware development services. Their chips are available with industrial temperature ranges. ICT’s pricing is competitive, making them a choice for budget‑sensitive projects. Despite their obscurity, ICT MCUs are widely deployed in Asian markets. They have a strong distribution network in China and Southeast Asia. ICT supports both assembly and C programming languages. Their microcontrollers are often pin‑compatible with other 8051 variants. ICT continues to innovate with new low‑power sleep modes. They also add watchdog timers and brown‑out detectors. ICT’s commitment to quality ensures long product lifecycles. Many legacy products rely on ICT chips, creating demand for reverse engineering.

ICT Security Features

ICT implements standard 8051 lock bits to secure the program memory. These lock bits, when set, prevent readback of the code. However, they can be bypassed through various techniques. For OTP parts, once programmed, the data is permanent and cannot be altered. This makes them resistant to malware injection but also prevents recovery if lost. ICT also offers a security fuse that disables external read commands. To extract firmware, we often need to decapsulate the die and probe the memory cells. Some ICT chips have a protection mechanism that erases the contents if tampered with. Despite this, we have successfully dumped OTP devices using UV light exposure to change bit states. ICT’s flash variants have sector lockdown features to prevent writing to critical areas. However, glitch attacks can sometimes unlock these sectors temporarily. The company uses a simple password scheme for JTAG access in some models. Cracking this password is feasible through brute‑force or side‑channel analysis. ICT’s security is generally weak compared to modern MCUs, making them easier to hack. We can recover full code dumps by exploiting the memory read timing. For OTP, we use a combination of chemical etching and micro‑probing to read each bit. This allows us to clone the device into a new flash chip. ICT also provides encrypted data transfer options, but they are rarely used. Many ICT chips lack a unique identifier, making clone detection impossible. This facilitates duplication for legacy system replacement. Our lab has developed efficient methods to attack the protection mechanism. We can unlock the chip within hours using fault injection. We also use power analysis to infer encryption keys when present. ICT’s security features are minimal, so extraction is straightforward. We have recovered firmware from over 100 ICT devices. The process involves read commands from the debug interface if unlocked. If locked, we proceed with invasive approaches. We also offer to recover data from damaged OTP cells. Our expertise ensures a high success rate. For clients needing to migrate away from ICT, we can duplicate the firmware onto modern microcontrollers. We also provide full disassembly and documentation. Trust MikaTech for all your ICT reverse engineering needs.
