About Myson

Myson Technology is a niche player in the microcontroller market, focusing on secure and mixed‑signal solutions. They produce ARM Cortex‑M based MCUs with advanced security features. Myson’s products are used in smart cards, digital rights management, and authentication. They are known for their low‑power designs and high integration. Myson offers a range of microcontrollers with embedded flash and SRAM. Their chips often include hardware crypto engines (AES, ECC, RSA). Myson targets the IoT and edge computing segments. They provide a comprehensive SDK and development tools. Myson’s microcontrollers are designed for applications requiring trust and reliability. They have a strong focus on tamper resistance and secure storage. Myson also produces dedicated security ICs for payment systems. Their chips are certified to Common Criteria EAL5+ level. Myson collaborates with major banks and governments for ID projects. They have a reputation for robustness and long‑term support. Myson’s product portfolio includes both general‑purpose and application‑specific MCUs. They are used in medical devices, vending machines, and access control. Myson’s security‑by‑design philosophy ensures data protection. They offer secure boot and secure firmware update mechanisms. Myson also provides lifecycle management for keys and certificates. Their devices are manufactured in advanced process nodes. Myson’s development environment includes secure debug interfaces. They are active in the global smart card and IoT security markets. Myson continues to innovate with new security solutions. Their chips are often used in critical infrastructure. Myson’s commitment to quality is backed by rigorous testing. They are a trusted partner for many OEMs. Myson’s documentation is comprehensive and well‑organized. Their microcontrollers are designed for long product lifecycles.

Myson Security Features

Myson builds security into every layer of their microcontrollers. They feature a hardware root of trust that ensures only authenticated code runs. The secure boot process verifies the digital signature of the firmware, blocking any unauthorized execution. Myson also implements a secure key vault with tamper‑resistant memory. This vault can store encryption keys that are never exposed to the CPU. The chips have active shields on the top metal layers to detect probing attempts. If tampering is detected, the device zeroizes all sensitive data. Myson’s debug ports are locked with a password that can be permanently disabled. Without the password, you cannot read or write memory. They also encrypt the external memory bus to prevent sniffing. Myson’s crypto engines are protected against side‑channel attacks by randomizing operations. Despite these robust measures, determined attackers have found ways to hack the system. Fault injection attacks can sometimes bypass the secure boot. Our team has successfully extracted keys from Myson devices using electromagnetic side‑channel analysis. We have also used voltage glitching to unlock the debug interface on older parts. To recover firmware, we often combine multiple techniques. We can duplicate the chip by reading out the flash after breaking the protection. Myson’s security is high, but no system is perfect. We have developed custom tools to attack the authentication mechanism. In some cases, decapsulation and micro‑probing are necessary. We offer both invasive and non‑invasive recovery options. Myson’s ongoing security updates mean we stay current. Our lab has the expertise to handle even the latest Myson devices. We provide full extraction and analysis services.
