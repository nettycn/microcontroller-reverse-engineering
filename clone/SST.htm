About SST

SST (Silicon Storage Technology) is a leading provider of SuperFlash® memory technology, which is used in many embedded microcontrollers. The company's IP is licensed to major semiconductor manufacturers, but SST also produces its own MCU products. SST's microcontrollers are known for their high‑endurance flash memory and low power consumption. They are often used in consumer electronics, automotive, and industrial applications. SST offers both 8‑bit and 32‑bit architectures, including 8051‑compatible cores and ARM‑based SoCs. Their SuperFlash® technology allows fast erase and program times. SST MCUs feature on‑chip EEPROM and SRAM for data storage. They support a variety of communication interfaces like SPI, I2C, and UART. SST also provides secure key storage and hardware encryption engines in some models. The company has a strong focus on cost‑effective solutions for high‑volume production. SST's products are widely used in smart meters, gaming devices, and medical equipment. They have a long history of innovation in non‑volatile memory. SST's flash memory is known for its reliability and data retention of over 100 years. They offer both industrial and automotive temperature grades. SST also provides development tools and reference designs. Their MCUs are often pin‑compatible with standard 8051 devices. SST has a global distribution network and technical support. They continuously improve their flash technology for lower voltage and higher speed. SST's memory cells are designed to withstand many program/erase cycles. The company is also involved in secure authentication ICs. SST's products are used in IoT devices for over‑the‑air updates. They offer a range of packages from QFN to BGA. SST's commitment to quality is reflected in their ISO certifications. They are a trusted supplier for many Fortune 500 companies. SST's MCUs are also found in automotive body electronics and infotainment. Their low‑power design makes them ideal for battery‑powered applications. SST continues to expand its portfolio with new advanced products.

SST Security Features

SST implements a range of security measures to protect firmware and data. Their 8051‑compatible devices feature lock bits that can disable external readback of the program memory. These bits are stored in non‑volatile configuration registers. Once set, they prevent any read or write access to the flash via the debug interface. However, glitch attacks on the power supply may temporarily unlock these protections. Some SST MCUs include a hardware encryption engine that can decrypt code on‑the‑fly, making it impossible to extract plaintext firmware without the key. The keys are stored in a secure memory area that is erased upon tamper detection. SST also provides a unique device identifier to prevent cloning of the chip. Their secure boot feature verifies the integrity of the firmware using digital signatures. This ensures only authenticated code runs, making it difficult to hack the boot sequence. Fault injection via clock glitching can be used to bypass signature checks in some older models. Side‑channel power analysis has been demonstrated to recover encryption keys from SST devices. Our lab has successfully recovered keys from SST32F series by measuring power consumption during AES operations. We have also used laser fault injection to alter the program counter and dump the flash contents. For legacy 8051 parts, we can unlock the lock bits using a high‑voltage programming technique. This allows us to read the full firmware and duplicate it onto a new device. SST's newer products include active shielding on the die to hinder micro‑probing. They also have a watchdog timer that can detect anomalous frequency deviations. Despite these measures, advanced attackers can still extract the bitstream by decapsulating the chip and using a scanning electron microscope. Our team offers both invasive and non‑invasive extraction services. We can recover the firmware from locked devices with a high success rate. SST's security is considered medium‑grade, suitable for consumer applications. For high‑security needs, SST recommends using their secure authentication companion chips.
