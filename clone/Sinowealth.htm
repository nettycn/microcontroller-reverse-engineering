About Sinowealth

Sinowealth is a Chinese semiconductor company known for its 8051‑compatible microcontrollers and touch‑sensing solutions. Their products are widely used in consumer electronics, home appliances, and industrial controls. Sinowealth offers a broad portfolio including general‑purpose MCUs, capacitive touch controllers, and wireless SoCs. They are one of the leading suppliers in the Asian market. Sinowealth's MCUs feature integrated flash memory, EEPROM, and SRAM. They provide low‑power modes for battery‑operated devices. Sinowealth also offers dedicated touch‑key chips with high sensitivity and noise immunity. Their devices support multiple interfaces like UART, SPI, I2C, and CAN. Sinowealth has a strong R&D team and holds many patents. They provide comprehensive development tools and software libraries. Their products are known for their cost‑effectiveness and ease of use. Sinowealth also supplies secure microcontrollers for payment terminals and access control. They have a partnership with ARM for certain product lines. Sinowealth's manufacturing uses advanced process nodes. They offer both industrial and automotive‑grade products. Their MCUs are used in smart home devices, power tools, and medical monitors. Sinowealth has a wide distribution network in China, India, and Southeast Asia. They provide excellent technical support and application notes. Sinowealth's touch solutions are used in white goods and automotive infotainment. They are also expanding into IoT with BLE and Zigbee products. Sinowealth's commitment to quality is reflected in their ISO 9001 certification. They continuously invest in new product development. Sinowealth is a reliable partner for high‑volume production. Their microcontrollers are often found in set‑top boxes and gaming peripherals. Sinowealth also offers OTP (One‑Time Programmable) options for fixed firmware. Their competitive pricing and performance make them a preferred choice for budget‑conscious designs.

Sinowealth Security Features

Sinowealth incorporates several security mechanisms to protect its MCUs. Most devices have lock bits that can be programmed to prevent readback of flash memory. These bits are stored in a dedicated configuration register. Once locked, the debug interface is disabled, and the code cannot be read out. However, it is possible to unlock them using a specific sequence of commands or by exploiting voltage glitches. Some Sinowealth MCUs feature a hardware encryption unit that can decrypt the firmware during boot. The decryption key is held in a secure area that is not accessible via standard read commands. This makes it difficult to extract the plaintext code. Sinowealth also provides a unique device ID to prevent cloning of the chip. They offer a secure bootloader that verifies the firmware signature. This ensures that only authenticated code runs, thwarting attempts to hack the system. Fault injection attacks, such as clock or power glitching, can sometimes bypass these checks. Side‑channel power analysis has been shown to reveal encryption keys from Sinowealth devices. Our lab has successfully recovered keys from SH32F series using differential power analysis. We have also used laser fault injection to alter the execution flow and dump the flash. For older OTP parts, we can read the contents by decapsulating the die and using a microscope. Sinowealth's security is adequate for most consumer applications but not for high‑security environments. They have added tamper detection and memory scrambling in newer devices. These features make it harder to recover the firmware. Nevertheless, our team has developed techniques to attack even the latest devices. We offer both invasive (FIB, probing) and non‑invasive (side‑channel, glitching) extraction services. We can clone the entire firmware and duplicate it onto a new chip. Our methods are proven and reliable. We can also recover data from damaged or partially erased devices. Contact us for a professional assessment of your Sinowealth MCU.
